# Query-independent topology gradients

The failed native allocation audits compare the weighted mean of sampled pair gradients with the gradient of the exhaustive target at the same fixed model parameters. Both pre-correction audits failed that comparison. Their original custom assertion hid the numerical discrepancy; the diagnostic capsule now preserves per-family statistics and gradient tensors. The completed Senseiver diagnostic isolates its failure to Shape: four gradient entries exceed the unchanged tolerance, while Scale has no mismatches. The four entries belong to the CH4 output row of the final head weights and bias, with tolerance multiples from 1.019 to 1.818; Shape relative gradient L2 discrepancy is `9.9280496822e-6`. This identifies the affected family but does not prove the correction resolves the native comparison until the corrected audit is executed.

The sliced persistence distance projects augmented birth/death points onto fixed directions, sorts each projection and sums absolute differences. Its derivative is nonsmooth at ties. Previously, unstable sorting could select different tie derivatives when unrelated larger diagrams changed the padded batch width. Matrix multiplication could also round a projection differently at different enclosing matrix shapes, changing the selected absolute-value derivative at an apparent tie. Identical scalar distances therefore did not imply identical sampled-leaf gradients.

The CPU reproduction uses strictly positive-persistence diagrams and a shared zero-initialized parameter head. Scoring one diagram alone versus beside larger diagrams fails the original gradient tolerance in 48 of 80 float64 cases and 37 of 80 float32 cases. Stable sorting alone leaves 15 float64 failures. Stable sorting together with explicit two-component projection arithmetic gives exact alone/padded and scalar/batched gradients in all 80 tested cases of each dtype. These are numerical derivative examples, not population-level model or efficiency measurements. The immutable scripts, timings and results are indexed under the campaign's `artifacts/allocation/oracle_cpu_diagnostics_v1/` directory.

Let a fixed leaf derivative be `g(i,j;theta)`, with uniform outer target weight `1/K` and inner weight `w(j)`. The required target derivative is `G(theta) = sum_i sum_j w(j) g(i,j;theta) / K`. For multinomial outer counts `N(i)` over `D` draws with positive proposal `p(i)`, and one shared inner draw `J(i)` per selected outer ID, the estimator is `sum_i N(i) g(i,J(i);theta) / (D K p(i))`. Its expectation equals `G(theta)` when the leaf derivative is fixed independently of the other sampled queries. Duplicate outer draws share their inner derivative; that correlation remains part of the exact variance calculation. Complete anchors and enumerated added leaves are fixed work and are not removed from the measured complete-update cost.

The production correction uses stable projection sorting and the same explicit two-term arithmetic for every point, independent of padding. It preserves the mathematical finite-angle distance while defining a consistent tie-subgradient choice; floating values may differ by roundoff. Full-model and residual parameter gradients inherit the same linear expectation at fixed parameters through their respective endpoint Jacobians. The corrected native audit must still pass the unchanged elementwise tolerance; the CPU examples do not replace that requirement.
